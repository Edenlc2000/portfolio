The brand

Project 32 is an electronic music brand taking dance music to every county in Ireland. I started it with no budget, no team and no audience.

I built the identity, the socials and the tone of voice, then made every asset that carried it: announcement films, event content, campaign copy and community comms. Eight events delivered, a 14,000-person database, a 60,000-strong social following, and not a euro of ad spend behind any of it.

Alongside it I run ChildsMind as an artist project, so I have built two brands in the same space at once and learned what transfers between them.

Directing a shoot

I have directed teams of up to four videographers across a single event, running multicam coverage and drone alongside it, then cut the whole thing together into full-set films.

The reason that works is not that I own the kit. It is that I already know exactly what footage the edit needs before anyone presses record: which angles carry the drop, where the crowd shot has to come from, what the drone has to catch and when. So I can brief a team on the night, in the dark, and know the cut exists before we leave.

Same instinct, either scale: I run the fast, phone-native content that converts, and I can direct a full crew when a job is bigger than one person.

Working to a brief

Every campaign here ran to a fixed date that could not move. I work backwards from launch, keep the tracker, and brief and manage the photographers, videographers and designers whose assets land in it.

That means coordinating venues, suppliers, artists, crew and brand partners across concurrent projects, then reporting on what the campaign actually did. Same-night turnaround is the standard, not the exception: footage from a Saturday is cut and live before Sunday.

How I shoot

My strongest results have come from UGC: fast, native, phone-shot content that reads as real to the people watching it. Every film here was made that way, and together they account for the numbers on this page.

I also shoot and edit on DSLR and mirrorless where a brief needs a higher finish, and I operate drone. Knowing which register a brief calls for is most of the skill: a polished spot in a UGC slot dies, and a phone edit in a brand campaign undersells it.

On software, CapCut is where I am fastest and most of this was cut there. I am working in Premiere now and will be fluent in it by the time I start.

Why it works

Because I am in the room every week. I DJ, I produce, and I am across what is moving in electronic music before any of it reaches a trend report. That is the difference between using a trend and knowing why it landed.

The rest is discipline. I built a content formula from my own performance data, watched views fall 86% when I drifted off it, went back to the numbers and rebuilt around what actually worked. Shoot, post, measure, adjust, every week.
